How should we source authentic queries for the remaining 29, so the benchmark reflects what researchers, teachers and the public actually ask?

DEN-Q01 example

What kinds of music did people make in the incarceration camps, and how did bands and dances contribute to their social lives?

32 sources frozen: 21 interview segments, 9 photos or ephemera, 1 newspaper issue, 1 documentary record.

We include hard negatives to test whether models can distinguish activities that happened inside the camps from similar activities that happened after incarceration.

1. Build the evidence base

What: for each query, a fixed set of candidate sources: interview segments, photographs, documents, films. How: search the collection; read every segment in full and inspect every image; deliberately add near-misses and distractors; freeze the set under a version number. Why: a fixed, fully judged set makes models comparable and prevents scoring well by luck or keyword overlap.

Kinds of reference points

Core Content: Does the answer include the key information? Required Qualifications: Does it preserve historical context and correct attribution? Conditional Points: If a specific topic is mentioned, is it handled accurately? Visual Points: Does the answer correctly use visual evidence? (Condition B only)

Designed traps

We include historically similar but distinct sources to test whether models make unsupported connections.

Example: Confusing the Matinee Orchestra with the Hawaiian Serenaders is flagged as a Critical Error (CE1).

Question for the Board

Who should validate ground truth for oral history collections? What counts as a serious misrepresentation of a narrator, and are our traps and required qualifications the right ones?

2. Establish the ground truth

What: decide what a correct output contains before any model runs. How: grade every source for relevance (2 relevant, 1 partial, 0 not relevant); write a reference answer and break it into reference points; link each point to the exact quote or image region that supports it. Why: outputs are judged against documented evidence, not against an evaluator's impression. Status: developed; needs expert validation.

Two input conditions

AM: transcripts and catalog text, no images. B: the same plus the image files. Comparing B with AM isolates the contribution of images. Caution: a score difference alone does not prove visual understanding. Questions answerable only from the images provide that evidence.

Question for the Board

Is this a credible test of whether models use images? How should the later audio, video and timestamp queries be designed, and which additional open models should we include?

3. Run models under controlled conditions

What: every model gets identical prompts and the same frozen sources. How: 3 models (ChatGPT, Gemini, a vision-capable LLaMA) x 3 prompting strategies (zero-shot, few-shot, chain-of-thought) x 2 input conditions. Tasks: retrieval, question answering, summary, and short image questions. Why: differences can be traced to the model, the strategy or the input, not to changed materials. Status: inputs prepared; model versions proposed; no runs yet.

What the framework measures

The proposal's four evaluation dimensions, made concrete:

  • Grounding and citation accuracy: are claims supported by the cited sources?
  • Hallucination: unsupported or contradicted claims, including designed traps
  • Coverage: does the answer include the required content?
  • Interpretive fidelity (the bias dimension): are narrators represented faithfully?
  • Privacy: what data leaves the institution? Plus cross-modal evidence use: do images add anything beyond text?

ORAL-AI benchmark methodology, piloted on DEN-Q01

The benchmark: 30 queries across three collections (Densho, Civil Rights History, Veterans History). Each is tested on retrieval, question answering and summarization, with ChatGPT, Gemini and LLaMA under three prompting strategies (Task 1.2). DEN-Q01 (music, bands and dances in the incarceration camps) is the first item built end to end. It serves here as the worked example of the method. Status: framework developed; expert validation pending; no model evaluations have been run.

4. Score each output

What: Evaluate model outputs against predefined ground truth. How:

  • QA & Summarization: Score reference-point coverage (0–2) and check individual claims for factual support.
  • Retrieval: Use relevance grades (0–2) to calculate P@5 and nDCG@5.
  • Errors: Record unsupported claims, incorrect citations, critical errors, and bias separately. Why: Identify not just how well a model performs, but where and why it makes mistakes. Status: Scoring framework prepared; model evaluation pending.

How We Score Model Outputs

  • Each claim: supported, partial, unsupported or contradicted
  • Each citation: correct or not
  • Reference Point Coverage: 0 = Missing/Wrong, 1 = Partial, 2 = Complete & Accurate
  • Critical errors, fidelity checks: yes or no, each
  • Image questions: 0, 1 or 2
  • Retrieval: ranking quality and precision, computed automatically and compared with chance
  • Privacy: Track where data is sent and whether it is stored by the model provider.

Example: scoring one reference point

Point: dances were frequent and usually held in mess halls. 2: "Narrators describe dances every week or two, mostly in mess halls cleared of tables [T02, T07]." 1: "There were dances in camp [T07]." (correct but incomplete) 0: the point is missing or wrong. Illustrative answers written for this map, not model outputs.

What the Scores Tell Us

  • Supported Claims: Percentage of claims supported by evidence.
  • Hallucination Rate: Percentage of claims that are unsupported or contradict the evidence.
  • Citation Accuracy: Percentage of claims citing the correct sources.
  • Core Coverage: How well the model covers the expected key information.
  • Visual Coverage: How accurately the model uses information from images.
  • Critical Errors & Bias: Serious historical mistakes and misrepresentations, reported separately so they are not hidden by high scores.

Question for the Board

Is a 0 / 1 / 2 scale fine-grained enough for oral history answers? Should critical errors and fidelity problems stay separate from coverage, as we propose, or be combined into one score?

5. Make the Scoring Reliable

What: Reduce subjectivity in human evaluation. How: Two independent blind scorers evaluate each output using the same scoring guide. We measure their agreement and resolve disagreements through adjudication. Why: Ensure that scoring is consistent and not dependent on one person's judgment.

Status: Scoring protocol prepared; not yet tested.

Expert Validation

Before scoring model outputs, domain experts will review and validate our ground truth to ensure its accuracy.

Scaling to 30 queries

Human double scoring for the pilot. An automated judge may assist later, but only for criteria where it agrees with human scorers at least as well as they agree with each other. Critical errors and fidelity stay with people.

Question for the Board

Is blind double scoring with adjudication the right standard for this domain? Under what conditions would you trust automated scoring of oral history answers?

6. Interpret the Results

Goal: Determine whether higher scores reflect meaningful improvements in model performance.

How:

  • Compare retrieval with random selection and keyword search.
  • Examine accuracy, coverage, hallucinations, and critical errors separately.
  • Check whether images provide real benefits beyond text.
  • Consider the limitations of a single-query pilot.

Why: A higher score alone does not prove that a model is more accurate, reliable, or better at understanding images.

What one pilot item cannot show

  • Which model is best
  • Audio, video and timestamp skills (planned for later queries)
  • Bias across demographic groups (needs comparisons across collections)
  • Memorization risk (needs a separate test)

What the pilot is for

Testing the protocol (workflow, scoring reliability, feasibility) before the full benchmark, not comparing models.
